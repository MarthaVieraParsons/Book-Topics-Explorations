# Chapter 3: Get Your Finances Buyer-Ready

Of all the chapters in this book, this might be the one that saves you the most heartache. I've seen buyers fall in love with a home only to discover their financing wasn't where they thought it was. Getting your finances buyer-ready *before* you start touring homes isn't just smart — it's what lets you shop with confidence instead of anxiety.

## Know Your Credit

Your credit score affects your interest rate, and even a modest difference in rate can mean tens of thousands of dollars over the life of a loan. Before you do anything else:

- Pull your credit reports from all three bureaus and review them for errors — they're more common than you'd think.
- Pay down revolving debt where you can; it's often the fastest lever to move your score.
- Avoid opening new credit accounts or making large purchases in the months leading up to your home search — lenders watch for exactly this.

## Know Your Real Budget

"What can I qualify for" and "what should I actually spend" are two different numbers, and conflating them is one of the most common buyer mistakes I see. A lender will tell you the maximum you qualify for based on income and debt ratios. That number rarely accounts for the lifestyle you actually want to live alongside your mortgage payment — travel, hobbies, helping family, simply having breathing room.

Build your own number first, based on your real monthly budget, before you ever talk to a lender. Then let the lender's number serve as a ceiling, not a target.

## Understand What "Pre-Approved" Really Means

Buyers often use "pre-qualified" and "pre-approved" interchangeably, but they're not the same thing:

- **Pre-qualification** is a quick, informal estimate based on self-reported information. It's useful for early planning but carries little weight with sellers.
- **Pre-approval** involves a lender actually verifying your income, assets, and credit, and it results in a conditional commitment for a specific loan amount. This is what you need in hand before you start making offers — especially in a competitive market where sellers expect it.

Get pre-approved early, even before you're sure you've found "the" home. It tells you your real number, surfaces any financing issues while there's time to address them, and lets you move quickly and confidently the moment the right home appears.

## Don't Forget the Other Numbers

Your mortgage payment is only part of the picture. Budget realistically for:

- Property taxes and homeowners insurance (we'll go deep on Southern Arizona specifics in the next chapter)
- HOA dues, if applicable
- A maintenance reserve — a good rule of thumb is one to two percent of the home's value annually
- Closing costs, typically two to five percent of the purchase price

## Your Readiness Checklist

Before moving to the next chapter, you should be able to check off:

- [ ] I've reviewed my credit reports and addressed any errors
- [ ] I know my real, comfortable monthly budget — not just my qualifying number
- [ ] I've started the pre-approval process with a lender
- [ ] I've budgeted for taxes, insurance, and ongoing maintenance, not just the mortgage

With this foundation in place, you're ready to explore how to actually finance the purchase — which is exactly where we're headed next.
