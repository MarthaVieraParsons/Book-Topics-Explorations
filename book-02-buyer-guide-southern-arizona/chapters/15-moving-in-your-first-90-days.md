# Chapter 15: Moving In: Your First 90 Days

Closing day isn't the finish line — it's the starting line for actually living here. This chapter covers the practical logistics of your first three months, so settling in feels smooth rather than scattered.

## Week One: The Essentials

- **Utilities** — confirm electric, water, gas, and internet are active in your name; in Arizona, getting air conditioning service transferred and functioning should be at the very top of this list, any time of year
- **Change your address** — with the post office, your bank, insurance providers, and anywhere else that matters
- **Locate your main shut-offs** — water, electrical panel, and gas (if applicable) — before you ever need them in an emergency
- **Test your HVAC system thoroughly**, even if it was inspected, so you understand how it runs before the weather gets demanding

## Weeks Two Through Four: Getting Oriented

- Register your vehicle and update your driver's license if you've relocated from out of state
- Find your nearest healthcare providers — primary care, urgent care, and pharmacy — before you need them, not during an emergency
- Locate the closest grocery store, hardware store, and other daily essentials
- If you're in an HOA community, attend an orientation or introductory meeting if one's offered, and get a copy of the community rules if you don't have one already

## Month Two: Building Routine

- Start exploring beyond your immediate neighborhood — this is when many of my clients begin discovering the restaurants, trails, and spots that become their regular favorites
- If you're new to the desert climate, learn your home's specific rhythms — when the sun hits which rooms, how the landscaping responds to watering schedules, how the home feels in full summer heat if you haven't experienced it yet
- Address any lingering move-in items from your inspection or closing that weren't urgent but shouldn't be forgotten

## Month Three: Building Community

- This is typically when the "new town" feeling starts to shift into something more like home
- Explore local clubs, classes, or groups tied to your interests — Southern Arizona has a strong culture of community organizations, especially in retirement-oriented areas
- If you came from a very different climate, this is often when the lifestyle benefits you moved here for — fuller use of outdoor space, more time outside, a different daily rhythm — really start to click into place

## A Few Honest Notes

Some adjustment is normal, even for people who are thrilled with their decision. New routines, new systems, a new climate — give yourself permission for an adjustment period rather than expecting everything to feel effortless immediately.

And if something about your new home or community isn't working the way you expected, reach out. I stay in touch with clients well past closing specifically because the relationship doesn't end when the transaction does — and plenty of small issues are much easier to solve with a quick conversation than they seem on their own.
