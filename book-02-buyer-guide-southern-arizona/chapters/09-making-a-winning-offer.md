# Chapter 9: Making a Winning Offer in Today's Market

Finding the right home is only half the job — getting your offer accepted, on terms that protect you, is the other half. This chapter breaks down how to build an offer that's genuinely competitive without overpaying or giving up protections you shouldn't.

## Price Isn't the Only Lever

Buyers often assume winning an offer is purely about the highest number. In reality, sellers weigh several factors together:

- **Price**, obviously
- **Financing strength** — a strong pre-approval or cash offer reduces the seller's risk of the deal falling through
- **Timeline** — how quickly you can close, and how well that matches the seller's own needs
- **Contingencies** — how many conditions stand between acceptance and a guaranteed closing
- **Earnest money** — a larger deposit signals seriousness and commitment

A well-structured offer uses all of these levers together, not just price alone.

## Understanding Contingencies

Contingencies protect you, but each one also makes your offer slightly less attractive to a seller in a competitive situation. The most common:

- **Inspection contingency** — lets you back out or renegotiate based on what an inspection reveals. I rarely recommend waiving this entirely, even in a hot market; there are ways to structure it (like a shortened inspection period) that stay competitive without giving up this protection outright.
- **Appraisal contingency** — protects you if the home appraises below the purchase price. We'll talk through your options if this happens in Chapter 10.
- **Financing contingency** — protects you if your loan falls through for reasons outside your control.
- **Sale-of-current-home contingency** — relevant if you need to sell an existing property to complete this purchase; this one meaningfully weakens an offer in a competitive market, which is why timing (covered in Chapter 11 for sellers, and relevant here too) matters so much.

## Reading the Room

Part of what I bring to this process is reading how competitive a specific listing actually is — not just what's "normal" for the market overall. A home that's been sitting for three weeks calls for a different strategy than one that hit the market yesterday with five showings already scheduled. We'll talk through the specific situation for any home you're serious about before we write an offer.

## Writing a Personal Touch, Carefully

Buyer's letters to sellers have become common, and they can occasionally help — but they can also introduce legal risk around fair housing if not handled carefully, and some listing agents actively discourage them for exactly that reason. I'll advise you on whether this makes sense for a specific situation.

## What Happens After You Submit

Once we submit your offer, one of three things happens:

1. **Acceptance** — straightforward, and we move to opening escrow.
2. **Counteroffer** — the seller proposes different terms, and we decide together how to respond.
3. **Multiple offers** — if you're in a competitive bidding situation, I'll walk you through your realistic options and help you decide your true ceiling in advance, so you're never making that decision under pressure in the moment.

## The Most Important Rule

Never let yourself get emotionally locked into a number that exceeds what you decided, in a calm moment, you were truly comfortable paying. I'll help you set that ceiling before we ever submit an offer — and I'll remind you of it if a bidding situation starts to pull you past it.
