# Chapter 1: Is Now the Right Time to Buy?

Almost every buyer asks me some version of the same question before they ask anything else: "Is now actually a good time to buy?" It's a fair question, and it deserves a real answer instead of the easy, salesy one.

Here's the honest truth: there is no universally perfect time to buy a home. There's only the right time *for you*, based on your finances, your timeline, and what you're actually trying to solve for. My job in this chapter isn't to convince you to buy right now — it's to give you a clear-eyed framework so you can answer that question for yourself with confidence.

## The Market Noise vs. Your Reality

Every year brings headlines about rates rising, rates falling, inventory tightening, inventory loosening. It's easy to feel like you need to time the market perfectly or you've missed your window. In practice, very few buyers — and even fewer sellers — ever truly "time" a market. What matters far more is whether a home works for your budget and your life *today*, because you can always refinance a rate, but you can't refinance a missed opportunity to buy the right home at the right value for your needs.

That said, it's worth understanding a few real market fundamentals that do matter:

- **Interest rates** affect your monthly payment, not your home's long-term value. A slightly higher rate on the right home still beats a lower rate on the wrong one.
- **Inventory levels** affect your negotiating leverage. In a tighter market, you'll need to move faster and present stronger offers; in a looser market, you have more room to negotiate price and terms.
- **Seasonality** matters in Southern Arizona specifically — winter months bring more competition from snowbirds and seasonal buyers, while summer tends to be quieter with more room to negotiate.

## The Questions That Actually Matter

Instead of asking "is the market right," ask yourself these:

1. **Is my financial foundation solid?** Do I have a handle on my credit, my savings, and what I can comfortably afford — not just what I can technically qualify for?
2. **Do I know how long I plan to stay?** Buying generally makes more financial sense the longer you plan to stay in a home, since it gives you more time to build equity and absorb transaction costs.
3. **Is my "why" strong enough to carry me through the process?** Whether it's relocating for a better quality of life, downsizing into something more manageable, or finally making Southern Arizona permanent after years of visiting — a clear motivation will carry you through the inevitable bumps in the process.

If your answers to these point toward "yes," the macro market conditions matter far less than you'd think.

## A Word on Waiting

Some buyers wait for the "perfect" moment — the lowest rate, the lowest price, the calmest market — and that moment rarely announces itself clearly enough to act on. I've watched buyers wait a full year for conditions to improve, only to find that home prices moved more than the savings they were waiting for. That's not a reason to rush into a decision you're not ready for. It is a reason to stop waiting for permission from the market and start preparing your own readiness instead.

That's exactly what the next two chapters will help you do.
