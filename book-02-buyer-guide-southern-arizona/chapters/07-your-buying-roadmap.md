# Chapter 7: Your Buying Roadmap: From Search to Keys

By now you've done the hardest internal work — getting financially ready, understanding your options, and narrowing in on where you want to be. This chapter lays out exactly what happens next, step by step, so nothing along the way feels like a surprise.

## Step 1: Finalize Your Pre-Approval

If you haven't already, lock in your full pre-approval with a lender. This isn't optional in today's market — sellers and their agents expect to see it attached to any serious offer.

## Step 2: Define Your Must-Haves vs. Nice-to-Haves

Sit down and separate your true must-haves (number of bedrooms, general location, budget ceiling) from your nice-to-haves (a pool, a specific view, a particular finish level). This list keeps your search focused and keeps you from talking yourself out of a genuinely good home over something minor.

## Step 3: Start Touring with Purpose

Once we start touring homes together, I'll help you evaluate not just how a home shows, but how it actually functions for your life — storage, layout flow, orientation to the sun (which matters more here than almost anywhere), and the realistic cost of any updates it needs.

## Step 4: Make an Offer

When you find the right home, we'll build a competitive offer together — price, terms, contingencies, and timeline — based on current market conditions and the specific property's circumstances. We'll go deeper on offer strategy in Chapter 9.

## Step 5: Negotiate

Few offers are accepted exactly as submitted. Expect some back-and-forth on price, repairs, closing timeline, or included items. This is a normal, healthy part of the process, not a sign anything has gone wrong.

## Step 6: Open Escrow and Schedule Inspections

Once your offer is accepted, we open escrow, and your earnest money deposit goes into a neutral third-party account. You'll schedule your home inspection (and any specialty inspections) during your due diligence period — more on this in Chapter 10.

## Step 7: Appraisal

If you're financing, your lender will order an appraisal to confirm the home's value supports the loan amount. We'll talk through what happens if the appraisal comes back lower than expected.

## Step 8: Clear Remaining Loan Conditions

Your lender may request additional documentation during underwriting. Respond promptly — delays here are one of the most common causes of closing timeline slippage.

## Step 9: Final Walkthrough

Shortly before closing, we'll walk through the home together one last time to confirm its condition matches what was agreed, and that any negotiated repairs were completed.

## Step 10: Closing Day

You'll sign your final loan documents, wire or deliver your closing funds, and the title officially transfers. In Arizona, this is typically handled through a title company and often does not require your presence to be exactly simultaneous — we'll make sure you know exactly what your closing day looks like well in advance.

## Step 11: Keys in Hand

The home is yours. We'll talk about your first 90 days as a new Southern Arizona homeowner in Chapter 15.

## What This Timeline Actually Looks Like

From an accepted offer to closing, expect somewhere between 30 and 45 days for a financed purchase, and potentially as little as two to three weeks for a cash purchase. Every transaction has its own rhythm, but knowing this general shape in advance helps the whole process feel predictable instead of overwhelming.
