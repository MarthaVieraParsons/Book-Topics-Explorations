# Chapter 2: Rent, Buy, or Wait?

Not everyone reading this book should buy a home right now — and I'd rather tell you that honestly in Chapter 2 than let you find it out the hard way in Chapter 11. This chapter is a decision framework, not a sales pitch. Let's work through it together.

## The Three Paths

**Renting** makes sense when you need flexibility more than you need equity — if you're not sure yet which Southern Arizona community fits you, if your timeline here is genuinely uncertain, or if a major life change (a new job, a health situation, a family transition) is still unresolved. There's no shame in renting for a year while you get to know the region before committing. In fact, I encourage some clients to do exactly that.

**Buying** makes sense when you have reasonable clarity on your timeline (generally three-plus years in the home), a stable financial foundation, and a genuine sense of which lifestyle and community fits you — even if you haven't picked the exact house yet.

**Waiting** makes sense when your finances need more time to get ready — more savings, better credit, more stable income — or when a major decision (a job change, a sale of another property, a family circumstance) is still pending. Waiting isn't failure. It's often the most financially sound choice, and a good Realtor will tell you so even when it means you're not buying with them today.

## Relocation Buyers: A Special Case

If you're relocating to Southern Arizona from out of state, this decision gets an extra layer. I often recommend relocation buyers consider a short-term rental for their first few months — even just three to six — before buying. It lets you experience a full season, get a feel for which community actually suits your daily life, and avoid the common and expensive mistake of buying sight-unseen based on a single visit.

That said, this isn't a universal rule. Buyers who've vacationed in the region for years, or who've done extensive research and multiple visits across seasons, often buy with confidence right away — and that's valid too.

## Retirees and Downsizers: A Different Calculation

If you're retiring or downsizing, the rent-vs-buy math shifts. You likely already have a strong sense of the lifestyle you want, and the "getting to know the market" period matters less than it does for a relocation buyer exploring blind. Here, the bigger question is usually about timing your sale and purchase together — which we'll cover in depth in Chapter 11.

## A Simple Gut-Check

Ask yourself:

- Can I see myself staying in this region for at least three years?
- Is my financial situation stable enough to commit to a mortgage and the responsibilities of ownership?
- Have I either experienced the community I'm considering firsthand, or built in a plan to do so before committing?

If you answered yes to all three, you're likely ready to buy. If you hesitated on any of them, that hesitation is useful information — not something to override by force of excitement.

Whichever path you're on, the next chapter will help you make sure your finances are genuinely ready before you start house-hunting in earnest.
