# Chapter 4: Financing Options for Every Buyer

There's no single "right" way to finance a home — the right option depends on your situation, your goals, and sometimes your stage of life. Let's walk through the paths I see most often with Southern Arizona buyers.

## Conventional Mortgages

The most common path for most buyers. A conventional loan typically requires a credit score in the mid-600s or higher and a down payment anywhere from 3% to 20%+, with private mortgage insurance (PMI) required below 20% down. These loans offer competitive rates and flexible terms, and they're worth exploring first for most buyers with solid credit and stable income.

## FHA Loans

Backed by the Federal Housing Administration, FHA loans are popular with first-time buyers and those with less-than-perfect credit or smaller down payments (as low as 3.5%). The trade-off is mortgage insurance that typically lasts for the life of the loan, which can make them more expensive long-term than a conventional loan once you qualify for one.

## VA Loans

For eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and some surviving spouses, VA loans are often the single best financing option available — frequently requiring no down payment at all and no ongoing mortgage insurance. Southern Arizona, with its proximity to several military installations, has a meaningful population of VA-eligible buyers, and I always make sure veteran clients understand the full value of this benefit before considering anything else.

## Cash Purchases

Cash buyers skip financing entirely, which means a faster closing, a stronger negotiating position, and no interest costs. I see this often among retirees and downsizers who are using proceeds from a previous home sale. If you're in this position, it's still worth discussing with a financial advisor whether tying up that much capital in one asset makes sense for your broader financial picture — cash is powerful, but it isn't automatically the best choice for everyone who has it available.

## Retiree-Specific Options: Reverse Mortgages

For buyers 62 and older, a Home Equity Conversion Mortgage (HECM) — commonly known as a reverse mortgage — can sometimes be used to purchase a home with a substantial down payment while eliminating monthly mortgage payments entirely. This is a specialized tool with real trade-offs (it reduces the equity available to heirs, and it comes with its own fees and counseling requirements), so it deserves a conversation with both a financial advisor and a lender who specializes in these products before you commit. I raise it here only so you know it exists as an option worth exploring if you're asset-rich and income-conscious in retirement.

## Choosing the Right Lender

Whichever financing path fits you, the lender you choose matters nearly as much as the loan product itself. Look for:

- Local or regional lenders who understand Southern Arizona's market specifically
- Clear, responsive communication from the start — if they're slow to respond during the application process, that's a preview of what closing will feel like
- A willingness to walk through multiple scenarios with you, not just push you toward the first option they mention

I'm happy to introduce clients to lenders I trust and have worked with repeatedly — a good lender relationship is one of the most valuable parts of a smooth transaction.
