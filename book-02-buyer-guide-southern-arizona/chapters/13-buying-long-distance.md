# Chapter 13: Buying Long-Distance: A Relocation Buyer's Playbook

A meaningful share of my clients buy their Southern Arizona home while still living somewhere else entirely — sometimes having visited the region only once or twice, sometimes not at all before closing. It's absolutely possible to do this well. Here's exactly how.

## Start with Research, Not Showings

Before you ever schedule a trip (virtual or in-person), get genuinely familiar with the region from a distance:

- Review this book's neighborhood primer (Chapter 6) against your specific priorities
- Ask me directly about the communities you're considering — I'll give you an honest comparison, not a sales pitch for whichever area happens to have inventory right now
- Use mapping tools to understand realistic drive times to the amenities that matter most to you (healthcare, airport, family)

## Make the Most of a Single Visit

If you can only make one trip before buying, structure it deliberately:

- Visit multiple communities in the same trip, even ones you're not sure about, so you have real comparison points
- Drive the actual commute routes you'd use regularly, at a realistic time of day
- If possible, time your visit for a season you haven't experienced yet, especially if you've only seen the region in winter

## Virtual Tools Have Come a Long Way

Between video walkthroughs, detailed photography, floor plans, and live video tours I can walk you through personally, it's genuinely possible to evaluate a home thoroughly without being there in person. I regularly do live video walkthroughs for long-distance buyers, pointing out details a listing's photos won't show you — true natural light, actual noise levels, real condition of finishes.

## The Remote Offer and Closing Process

Buying remotely doesn't mean giving up protections:

- Offers can be signed and submitted entirely electronically
- Inspections can be attended by me or a trusted representative on your behalf, with a full video or detailed written report shared with you immediately
- Closings can typically be handled through remote online notarization or a local mobile notary, meaning you often don't need to travel for closing day itself

## Common Long-Distance Buyer Mistakes

In my experience, the mistakes I see most often with remote buyers are:

- **Buying based on a single visit during the "easy" season** (usually winter) without accounting for summer heat
- **Underestimating commute realities** that looked fine on a map but feel different in actual traffic patterns
- **Skipping or rushing due diligence** because of the logistics of being remote — when in fact remote buyers should lean on inspections and specialist reviews even more heavily, not less

## My Role as Your Eyes on the Ground

For long-distance clients, I see my role as being genuinely your eyes and ears here — flagging things you wouldn't think to ask about, giving you my honest read on a neighborhood's trajectory, and never letting distance become a reason for you to receive less complete information than a local buyer would get.

Buying from afar takes a bit more trust and a bit more communication, but it doesn't have to take more risk.
