# Chapter 11: Navigating Closing Without Surprises

Closing day should feel anticlimactic in the best possible way — the paperwork equivalent of crossing a finish line you've already basically won. This chapter makes sure it actually feels that way, instead of stressful.

## The Final Walkthrough

Typically scheduled within 24 to 48 hours of closing, the final walkthrough is your last chance to confirm:

- The home's condition matches what was agreed when you made your offer
- Any negotiated repairs were actually completed
- Nothing has changed since your last visit (appliances still present, no new damage)

This isn't the time to renegotiate the deal — it's a confirmation step. If something is genuinely wrong, we address it immediately, before closing proceeds.

## Understanding Closing Costs

Closing costs typically run 2% to 5% of the purchase price for buyers, and usually include:

- Loan origination and underwriting fees
- Appraisal and credit report fees
- Title insurance and escrow fees
- Recording fees
- Prepaid items like homeowners insurance and property tax reserves

Your lender is required to provide a Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing, detailing every cost. Review it carefully, and don't hesitate to ask questions about anything that looks different from your original estimate.

## The Paperwork, Demystified

You'll sign a substantial stack of documents at closing, but most buyers are surprised how manageable it feels when someone walks them through it in advance. The core documents include:

- **The promissory note** — your legal promise to repay the loan
- **The deed of trust or mortgage** — which secures the loan against the property
- **The Closing Disclosure** — the final, official breakdown of costs
- **The deed** — the document that actually transfers ownership to you

## How Funds Move

You'll need to deliver your closing funds — typically via wire transfer — to the title company before or on closing day. Wire fraud targeting real estate closings has become a real and serious risk industry-wide. **Never trust wiring instructions received only by email.** Always confirm wiring details by phone, using a number you already know is legitimate, before sending any funds. I'll walk every client through exactly how this works and how to verify it safely.

## What Happens on Closing Day Itself

In Arizona, closings are typically handled through escrow with a title company, and you often don't need to be in the same room as the seller. Depending on the transaction, you may sign documents in person, electronically, or through a mobile notary. Once all documents are signed and funds are received, the deed is recorded with the county, and ownership officially transfers to you.

## Right After Closing

Keep copies of every closing document in a safe, accessible place — you'll want them for tax purposes, insurance claims, and eventually, when you sell. I recommend both a digital and physical copy.
