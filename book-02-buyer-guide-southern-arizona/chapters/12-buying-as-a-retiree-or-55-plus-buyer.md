# Chapter 12: Buying as a Retiree or 55+ Buyer

Retirees make up a significant share of my clients, and they tend to arrive with a specific set of questions that are different from a younger buyer's. This chapter speaks directly to those questions.

## Age-Restricted vs. Age-Targeted Communities

It's worth understanding the distinction clearly:

- **Age-restricted (55+) communities** legally require at least one resident per household to be 55 or older, with limits on younger residents. These communities can enforce their amenity and lifestyle focus more consistently as a result.
- **Age-targeted communities** market toward retirees and active adults but don't carry legal age restrictions — you'll find a broader mix of ages, though the lifestyle and amenities may still appeal strongly to retirees.

Neither is inherently better — it depends on whether you specifically want the consistency of an age-restricted environment or simply want a retirement-friendly lifestyle without the restriction.

## What to Actually Evaluate in a 55+ Community

Beyond the amenities in the brochure, dig into:

- **HOA financial health** — ask for reserve study results and recent dues history, not just the current rate
- **Healthcare proximity** — how far is the nearest hospital and specialist care you may need, realistically, not just "nearby"
- **Social culture fit** — amenities matter less than whether the actual community of residents feels like your people; visiting during an active social event tells you more than any brochure
- **Resale patterns** — how has the community's value held up, and how quickly do homes typically sell there

## Should You Buy Before or After Selling Your Current Home?

This is one of the most common questions I get from retiree buyers, and the honest answer is: it depends on your risk tolerance and your current home's market.

- **Buying first** gives you certainty and avoids a rushed move, but may require a bridge loan or enough liquidity to carry two properties temporarily.
- **Selling first** gives you clarity on your exact budget and avoids carrying two mortgages, but may mean a temporary rental gap between homes.

We'll go much deeper on coordinating this exact situation if you're also selling a home — but know that there's no universally right answer, only the one that fits your specific financial comfort.

## Financing Considerations Unique to Retirees

Retirees sometimes worry that fixed or reduced income will complicate financing. In practice, lenders can count retirement income, Social Security, pension distributions, and investment income toward qualification — it just requires proper documentation. A lender experienced with retiree buyers will know exactly how to structure this; it's worth confirming your lender has that specific experience.

## A Note on Timing Your Move

Many of my retiree clients wait longer than they need to, treating the move as a single irreversible leap rather than a transition. It doesn't have to be all-or-nothing. Some clients buy here first as a part-time residence, build familiarity over a year or two of seasonal living, and then transition to full-time once they're certain. There's no prize for moving fastest — only for moving well.
