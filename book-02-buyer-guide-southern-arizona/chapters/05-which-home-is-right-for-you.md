# Chapter 5: Which Home Is Right for You?

Once your finances are in order, the next big decision is what kind of home actually fits your life. This isn't just about bedrooms and bathrooms — it's about maintenance, lifestyle, and how you want your days to feel.

## Single-Family Homes

The most flexible option, with the widest range of neighborhoods, price points, and styles. A single-family home gives you full control — your own lot, your own decisions about renovations and landscaping, no shared walls. The trade-off is that all the maintenance responsibility sits with you, which matters more for some buyers than others.

This is the right fit if you want maximum flexibility and don't mind (or actively enjoy) handling upkeep yourself, or hiring it out on your own terms.

## 55+ and Age-Restricted Communities

Southern Arizona, especially the Green Valley and Sahuarita corridor, has some of the country's most established 55+ communities. These typically offer low-maintenance living, extensive amenities (golf, clubhouses, fitness centers, organized social activities), and a built-in community of peers in a similar life stage.

This is the right fit if an active, social, low-maintenance retirement lifestyle is exactly what you're shopping for — and if you're comfortable with the community's age restrictions and often more structured HOA rules.

## Condos and Townhomes

Condos and townhomes offer a middle ground — less maintenance than a single-family home, often a lower price point, and sometimes amenities like shared pools or fitness facilities. HOA fees tend to be higher than a typical single-family HOA, since they cover more shared infrastructure, so it's worth reviewing those costs and rules carefully.

This is the right fit if you want a "lock and leave" lifestyle — ideal for snowbirds and buyers who travel frequently — without the responsibilities of a standalone home.

## Land and Custom Builds

For buyers who want something no existing home can offer, buying land and building custom is a real option in parts of Southern Arizona, particularly in the more rural stretches around Vail, Sahuarita, and the wider region. This path takes longer and requires more hands-on decision-making, but it delivers a home built precisely around how you want to live.

This is the right fit if you have a clear vision, the patience for a longer process, and ideally some experience (or a strong team) to guide you through construction.

## New Construction

Communities like Marana and parts of Oro Valley continue to see significant new construction, offering modern layouts, builder warranties, and the ability to select finishes. New construction often comes with a more predictable timeline than custom building, though it still requires patience and a clear understanding of the builder's contract terms.

This is the right fit if you want a brand-new home without the full custom-build process, and you don't mind being an early resident in a still-developing neighborhood.

## Matching Home Type to Life Stage

As a general pattern, I see:

- **Relocating families** lean toward single-family homes or new construction in growing areas like Marana and Vail.
- **Retirees and downsizers** often gravitate toward 55+ communities or low-maintenance condos.
- **Snowbirds** frequently prefer condos or townhomes for the lock-and-leave convenience.
- **Buyers with a strong vision** sometimes choose land and custom builds, regardless of life stage.

None of these are rules — just patterns worth knowing as you think through your own priorities.
